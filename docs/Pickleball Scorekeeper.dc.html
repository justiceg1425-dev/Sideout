1
Which side serves — presence
The bar exists only under the serving side. The receiving side's zone is empty. The serving side's digit is pure white; the receiver's drops to #5B5B60. Two channels, both contrast-only.
2
Server 1 or 2 — segment count
One solid bar for first server, two segments for second. A shape change large enough to read peripherally, and the only visual event on a rally that advances the server. Singles and rally scoring always draw the solid bar.
3
Court half — horizontal position
The bar docks to the right edge of its half when the serving score is even, the left edge when odd. The marker sits where the server stands. Nothing to decode: the picture is a diagram of the court.
Amber is redundant throughout. Strip the colour and every dimension still reads: bar present / absent, one piece / two, docked left / right. That is the sunlight test.

Reconnection
The phone replays state, not events. When several rallies arrive at once the scoreboard jumps straight to current and the voice speaks the latest callout only — never a queue of stale scores over a live point. If the gap crossed a side-out, the "Side out" prefix is dropped; it describes a moment that has already passed. Default is Latest only; Nothing is the alternative. There is no "announce all".
Speaker not connected
Stated once, in setup, as a fact rather than an error, and never again mid-game. Audio falls back to the phone speaker; the scoreboard says nothing about it. Losing the speaker mid-game is silent — the scoreboard is still doing its job.

Watch, visual
Digit swaps with a 140 ms vertical roll and a 1.0 → 1.08 → 1.0 scale punch. Serve bar hops to the other edge of its half, 180 ms spring. Nothing else moves.
Digits do not move at all. The single bar splits into two segments in place, 200 ms — the gap opens from the centre. This is the only screen event, so it cannot be mistaken for a point.
The bar travels the full width of the screen, across the gutter, to the other side's half, 320 ms ease-out — the largest motion in the app. Digit brightness swaps with it. Arriving bar is always a single segment.

.success — one crisp rising tap. Short, final, the reward beat.
.directionUp — two light taps, 70 ms apart. Two taps for second server; the count is the meaning.

Spoken
"Five, three, two." Serving score, receiving score, server number. Two numbers in singles and rally.
"Five, three, two." Identical numbers to the previous callout except the third — which is the whole point, and why the haptic and the bar carry the load.
"Side out. Three, five, one." Prefix first, then the score from the new serving side's perspective — so the numbers audibly flip.
Fires when
Serving side won the rally.
Receiving side won, doubles side-out, server was 1.
Receiving side won, and server was 2 — or singles, or the opening 0-0-2 server.
Haptics fire on the watch the instant the tap commits, from the watch's own copy of the engine. They never wait on the phone. If the phone is unreachable the felt experience is unchanged and only the voice is missing.
06
Two rationales
Why a bar and not a badge
A badge with "2" in it needs to be read. A bar is seen. Serve state has three dimensions and a badge can only carry them as text, which fails at two seconds, at arm's length, in sun, with a paddle in hand.

Position, count and presence are pre-attentive: the eye resolves them before it resolves a glyph, and all three survive glare, motion blur and the always-on dimmed state. The bar's horizontal placement is not a symbol for the court half — it is a picture of it, so there is nothing to learn and nothing to forget between games. Amber is decoration; delete it and the display still works.
Why scrubbing turns the screen cyan
A history view that looks like the live view is worse than no history view. The player will glance at it mid-argument and read a past score as the current one.

So the scrub state changes every channel at once: hue swings from amber to cyan, digits go hollow and dim, the tap regions stop accepting input, and a border appears around the whole screen. It is unmistakably a different mode. The tick ladder along the top is the rally array drawn literally — one tick per rally, the tall one is here, dim ticks ahead are the redo tail. Spinning back three and forward two is a physical motion along that ladder.

Committing is deliberate: the crown stops at the truncation point and a tap on either half resumes live scoring from there, discarding the redo tail. Idle for 6 s snaps back to live untouched — the safe default when the player gets distracted and just starts playing again.
